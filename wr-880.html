IMCI findings

RefFindingClausesSeverity
F1Motor power and top speed are wrong, so the manoeuvring test applies. The checklist gives fitted power as “2” kW and top speed as 40 km/h. The transom letter (D3) gives 2 × 300 HP = 447 kW, which is above Pcalc = 322,4 kW. The scantling summary (D2) gives a service speed of 40 kn (≈ 74 km/h), which is above the 48 km/h trigger. Either one makes the 7.3 maximal manoeuvring-speed test mandatory. “Put sticker” does not close 7.3. 447 kW is also above the 356 kW maximum engine power verified on Annex III CAIIIGEMI002, so the twin 300 HP installation is outside what IMCI confirmed.Correct the power and speed fields. Either limit the installed power to ≤ 356 kW or apply for an Annex III revision covering 447 kW. Supply the quick-turn or avoidance-line test record, the ISO 7010 W001 label, and evidence of the speed-measuring device.7.2, 7.3High
F2The ventilation sizing uses a tank volume that does not match the tank drawing. The ventilation report (D7) deducts 0,614 m³ for the fuel tank. The 300 L tank on drawing 350-000-031 (D9) has an outer envelope of 1,700 × 0,640 × 0,300 = 0,326 m³. With 0,326 m³, the net volume is about 0,794 m³, the required area is A = 3 300·ln(0,794/0,14) ≈ 5 725 mm², and the minimum ID is about 85,4 mm. On that basis both 75 mm and 80 mm fail. Clauses 4.13 (terminal fittings) and 5.2 (exhaust in the lower third) are also still “to be confirmed”.Explain where 0,614 m³ comes from or re-issue the calculation. Confirm the as-built duct ID (≥ 90 mm if 0,326 m³ is correct), the terminal-fitting area and the exhaust pick-up height.6.11High
F3Total buoyant volume has a near-zero margin and its breakdown is missing. V = 6,48 m³ is declared against 6,472 m³ required, a margin of 0,008 m³ (0,1 %). The tube (4,52) plus foam (0,46) plus watertight volume (0,34) from D5 add up to 5,32 m³, which leaves 1,16 m³ unaccounted for. If 5,32 m³ is the real total, 7.6.1 fails.Supply the buoyancy calculation (Annex III) with each item listed: tube, foam, sealed volumes and buoyant structure.7.6.1, 5.7ClosedBuoyancy and flotation (3.3) verified by IMCI on Annex III.
F4Remote steering is marked N/A. A twin-outboard Type X RIB with a console has remote steering, and 6.6 requires conformity to ISO 8848 or ISO 10592 plus manufacturer approval for Type X.Change to Pass or Fail and supply the steering system make, model and its conformity evidence.6.6High
F5Hull length is not consistent across documents. Annex III CAIIIGEMI002 verifies LH = 8,80 m and BH = 2,95 m, so 8,80 m stands and the ISO 15085 option is the one for LH ≥ 8,5 m. The values below are what still needs correcting. The checklist gives LH = 8,80 m. The ISO 11812 report (D6) gives LH = 7,88 m and BH = 2,22 m. D2 gives LOA 8,8 m and LWL 7,48 m. The difference matters for ISO 15085 Table 3, which splits at 8,5 m and therefore decides which man-overboard option applies.Re-issue the ISO 11812 report with LH 8,80 m to match Annex III. Use the Annex III values on the builder's plate.Data, 7.10, 8Low
F6Total tube length is entered as 8,80 m. LT is the tube length on all sides added together, probably about 17–18 m. With LT = 8,80 m the attachment test mass works out at 414,7 kg. With about 17,6 m it would be about 207 kg. A test at 414,7 kg is conservative and would still be acceptable.Correct LT and state the test mass actually used for the 7.14 drop/slide test, with the test record.7.14Medium
F7Drainage has passed the numbers but the report is not closed. D6 (Rev 0) states that quick-draining status “cannot be declared” until action A1 (basis for the 0,60 m sill criterion) and action A3 (clause 6.2 watertightness schedule for appliances in the recess) are closed. The draining-time margin is small (4,89 min against 5,00 min).Flooding (3.5) is verified by IMCI on Annex III. For the technical file, close A1 and A3 and issue Rev 1 of the drainage report.7.13Low
F8The scantling summary needs to be re-issued. In D2 the stiffener table enters the displacement as 10 728 (the lb value) in a kg field, and column D mixes imperial values in metre fields (28,87 / 7,22). The particulars show service speed as both 45 kn and 40 kn. Only bottom panels and stiffeners were assessed. The summary also recommends an extra CSM300 on the webs of stiffeners A and B.Re-issue with consistent units. Add the side, deck and transom-area panels or reference them, and confirm the extra CSM300 is in the production laminate.7.15ClosedStructure (3.1) verified by IMCI on Annex III (ISO 12215-5:2019). For the technical file, keep a clean copy of the scantling report.
F9Fire protection is marked N/A. The craft has a permanently installed 300 L petrol tank and 447 kW of outboard power. ISO 9094 is normally applicable.Give the basis for N/A, or assess against ISO 9094 (extinguisher provision, sizing and location).6.13Medium
F10The ISO 15085 option is not stated. Devices 2, 4, 5 and 6 are marked N/A without saying which Table 3 option (non-sailing, Cat B) was chosen. Annex III fixes LH at 8,80 m, so the option must be the one for LH ≥ 8,5 m.State the selected option and the hull length used to choose it.7.10 (15085)Medium
F11The fuel system has a drawing only. D9 gives the tank geometry and material, but nothing shows conformity to ISO 21487 (pressure test, marking, information plate) or ISO 10088 (hoses, filler, vent, earthing). The ventilation report quotes 0,614 m³, which does not match this tank (F2).Supply the tank pressure-test record, a photo of the tank label, and the hose and filler specification.6.10.2Medium
F12Liferaft stowage is marked N/A. For a Category B Type X craft, the basis for this needs to be stated.Show the liferaft stowage provision, or give the reason it does not apply.7.12Medium
F13Most evidence references are internal folder paths, not documents. Examples are “DATA & STANDARDS\35 - ISO Testing Pics & Video”, “ANNEX III's\WR 880\…”, “See Owners Manual” and “See pics - Peter”. None of these files were in the package. This covers the tube-fabric tests (5.2.2.x), tube tests (7.9.2.x), accessory strength (7.16), Annex III, the owner's manual and the builder's plate.Send the referenced files: fabric supplier certificates, test records, Annex III, owner's manual, plate photo, and the ISO 15084 load calculation (.xlsx).5.2, 7.4–7.9, 7.16, 8–10Low

Type & category
Type IX Powered boats fitted with a buoyancy tube covering at least 85% of the port and starboard sides, suitable for inshore and sheltered waters up to and including wind force 6 Beaufort and significant wave heights up to 2 m (design categories C and D), with a hull length between 8 m and 24 m and a motor power rating of 15 kW and greater.
Type X Powered boats fitted with a buoyancy tube covering at least 85% of the port and starboard sides, suitable for navigation up to wind force 8 Beaufort and significant wave heights up to 4 m (design category B), with a hull length between 8 m and 24 m and a motor power rating of 75 kW and greater.
Design category B — Notified Body required. A category B (Type X) craft cannot be assessed by internal production control alone. Under the Recreational Craft Directive 2013/53/EU the stability and design-and-construction assessment must involve a Notified Body. Stability is assessed to the latest ISO 12217-1.

Formula inputs. The material-test formulas take d as the maximum tube diameter in millimetres and p as the nominal pressure in bar. Tear resistance Ft takes the greater of the calculated value and the 75 N floor (5.2.2.5); seam strength Fs is ten times Ft. The attachment-mass formula matches the worked example in 7.14.3 (10 m boat, 10 000 kg, 20 m tube, 1 m sample gives 375 kg).

Symbols & terms — plain-language reference

click to collapse
Symbols used in the data fields
LH Hull length — length of the rigid hull, measured to ISO 8666 with the tubes at normal pressure. m
Lmax Maximum length — overall length including anything that projects, tubes at normal pressure. m
Bmax Maximum beam — the widest point, tubes at normal pressure. m
mLC Light craft mass — the finished boat empty of crew, cargo, fuel and stores (light craft condition, ISO 12217-1). kg
mLDC Loaded mass — the boat at its full rated load: crew, stores, fuel, engines, everything (loaded displacement condition, ISO 8666). kg
mT Trailer mass — mass of the boat as it sits on a trailer for towing. Used for the lifting-point design load. kg
Pcalc Calculated motor power — the reference maximum engine power for the boat's size, from length, beam and mass. If the fitted power is higher, the manoeuvring-speed test applies. kW
V Total buoyant volume — the whole volume that keeps the boat afloat: tubes plus any built-in foam, sealed air compartments and buoyant rigid parts. m³
Vc Compartment volume — the volume of one buoyancy-tube air chamber. m³
N Number of separate air compartments in the tube. Minimum five.
LT Total tube length — the buoyancy tube on every side of the boat, added together. m
LSTS Sample tube length — the length of the short off-cut of buoyancy tube used in the attachment strength test. You choose it; minimum 0,5 m. m
mt Test weight mass — the weight dropped in the attachment test, worked out from LSTS, LT and mLDC. kg
d Tube diameter — the largest diameter of the buoyancy tube, measured on the straight sections. mm
p Nominal pressure — the normal working pressure of the tube at 20 °C. bar
Ft Tear resistance — the minimum tearing force the tube fabric must withstand. N
Fs Seam strength — the minimum load a tube seam must hold. N
ALV Windage area — the side-on area of the hull the wind pushes against, at the loading assessed. Used in the stability check. m²
Key terms
RIB Rigid inflatable boat — a rigid lower hull with a soft buoyancy tube (inflated or foam-filled) along the sides, where the tube gives at least half of the buoyancy the boat needs.
Inflatable tube A buoyancy tube split into several separate air chambers, inflated with air, running along both sides.
Foam-filled tube A buoyancy tube filled with closed-cell foam instead of air.
Crew limit (CL) The largest number of people allowed on board when underway, as shown on the builder's plate.
Nominal pressure The correct everyday operating pressure for the tubes at 20 °C.
Significant wave height The average height of the highest third of the waves — the standard way sea state is described.
Category B — offshore Suited to seas up to 4 m wave height and wind up to Beaufort 8.
Category C — inshore Suited to seas up to 2 m and wind up to Beaufort 6.
Category D — sheltered Suited to waves up to 0,3 m (occasional 0,5 m) and wind up to Beaufort 4.
Type IX RIB with tube covering at least 85% of each side, categories C and D, hull 8–24 m, power 15 kW and greater.
Type X RIB with tube covering at least 85% of each side, category B, hull 8–24 m, power 75 kW and greater.
Builder's plate The permanent plate on the boat showing category, crew limit, maximum load and tube pressures.
WIN Watercraft Identification Number — the boat's unique identifying number.
Type test A one-off test on a representative boat or sample that qualifies the model, not repeated on every unit built.
